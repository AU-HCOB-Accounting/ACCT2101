⚠️ Why the First Four Chapters Are Everything

Chapters 1 through 4 Are the Foundation of This Entire Course

Every topic in this course builds on the concepts introduced in the first four chapters. If you struggle with Chapters 1–4, you will struggle with every chapter that follows. There is no way to catch up later—these concepts are cumulative.

Chapters 1 & 2 are especially critical. They introduce the Accounting Equation (A = L + E), double-entry bookkeeping, debits & credits, journal entries, and T-accounts. These are the building blocks of everything in accounting.

Your 1st Exam on May 28 covers Chapters 1 & 2 plus the Ch1-2 Practice Test. That is only 10 days away.

I am not exaggerating: students who invest serious time in Chapters 1–2 in the first week almost always perform well for the rest of the course. Those who delay usually fall behind and cannot recover in an 8-week format.

🎯 Ch1-2-Practice-Test — Your Most Important Preparation Tool

What Is It?

The Ch1-2-Practice-Test is an interactive HTML file (attached to this email) that tests your mastery of the core concepts from Chapters 1 and 2. It has three modules:

  • Module 1 — Account Classification: Classify 28 accounts as Asset, Liability, or Stockholders' Equity
  • Module 2 — Transaction Effects: Analyze 28 transactions and determine which accounts Increase or Decrease
  • Module 3 — Normal Balances: Identify whether each of 28 accounts has a normal Debit or Credit balance

How Much Is It Worth?

200 Points — 20% of Your Total Grade

The Ch1-2-Practice-Test is worth the same as your Comprehensive Final Exam. It is not optional. It is not just "practice." Your Test mode score from this tool is a major part of your 1st Exam grade on May 28.

How to Use It

  1. Open the file (ch1-2-practice-test.html) in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge.
  2. Enter your name and Student ID (your 9-digit ID starting with 912).
  3. Practice mode first (unlimited): Use Practice mode as many times as you want. Each wrong answer shows you a detailed explanation. There is no time pressure. This is where you learn.
  4. Practice with Timer (unlimited): Once you feel confident, try Practice+Timer mode. Each question has a time limit (4 seconds for Modules 1 & 3, 16 seconds for Module 2). This prepares you for the real test.
  5. Test mode (ONE attempt only): When you are ready, take the Test. It is timed and you get only one chance. Your results will be encrypted automatically.
  6. Copy & Email: After completing all three modules in Test mode, an encrypted result will be generated. Click the copy button to copy the encrypted text. Then send an email to sapark@augusta.edu with your name and Student ID in the subject line. In the email body, paste only the encrypted text—nothing else. Do not modify the encrypted text in any way.
⚠ Do NOT rush into Test mode. You only get one attempt. Practice extensively first. Once you can consistently score 90%+ in Practice+Timer mode, you are ready for the real Test.

How to Prepare

Here is my recommended preparation strategy:

  1. Study Chapters 1 & 2 using the lecture videos, transcripts, or study guide.
  2. Use the Accounting Equation Simulator (see below) to build intuition about how transactions work.
  3. Start the Practice Test in Practice mode. Read every explanation for questions you get wrong.
  4. Repeat Practice mode until you understand why each answer is correct, not just which answer is correct.
  5. Move to Practice+Timer mode and aim for 90%+ consistently.
  6. Take the Test when you are confident.
⚖️ Accounting Equation Simulator — Learn by Doing

What Is It?

The Accounting Equation Simulator is an interactive tool I built specifically for this course. It lets you enter business transactions and see in real time how each transaction affects the accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity).

Instead of just reading about how transactions work in a textbook, you can experience them. This is one of the most effective ways to understand the foundation of double-entry bookkeeping. The Simulator covers transactions through Chapter 4 (Completing the Accounting Cycle), including closing entries. I strongly recommend using it repeatedly as you progress through Chapters 1–4 to build and reinforce your intuition.

How to Use It

  1. Open the attached file (Accounting-Equation-Simulator.html) in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Enter the password: ACCT2101CO1
  3. Work through the guided transactions and observe how each one changes the equation.
  4. Pay attention to which accounts are affected and whether they increase or decrease.
Note: This tool is available only during the Summer 2026 semester. After the semester ends, the file will no longer be accessible. Take advantage of it now while you can.

Tip: Use the Simulator alongside your Chapter 1 & 2 study. After watching a lecture or reading about a concept, try it in the Simulator to reinforce your understanding.

🔄 Accounting Cycle Simulators — Practice Every Chapter

What Is It?

Building on the Accounting Equation Simulator, I’ve created a set of chapter-by-chapter simulators that cover the journal entries and ledger postings for the entire course (Chapters 1–13). Choose a simulator (Ch 1–2, Ch 3–4, Ch 5–8, or Ch 9–13), pick a worked example, and step through each transaction to see how it changes the accounting equation, how it is journalized, how it posts to the T-accounts, and how it shapes the financial statements.

These are ideal for exam preparation: each exam’s chapters have their own examples — purchases & sales, inventory costing (FIFO/LIFO/Weighted-Average), depreciation methods, bonds, stock issuance, dividends, and more.

How to Use It

  1. Open it here in Chrome or Edge: Accounting Cycle Simulators (also linked in the sidebar of every textbook page).
  2. Enter the password: ACCT2101CO1
  3. Pick a Simulator → a Chapter → an Example, then click Next (or use the ←/→ arrow keys) to step through.
  4. A 🔗 marks a linked sequence — transactions that build on one another (e.g., a purchase and its later payment or adjustment).
Note: Like the other tools, this is available only during the Summer 2026 semester.
