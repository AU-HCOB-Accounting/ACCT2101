Chapter 3 — The Adjusting Process

📖 For worked examples and full detail, see the Study Guide — Chapter 3.

The alternative treatment of deferrals (Appendix 3A) is recognized but not heavily tested.

Key Ideas

  • Adjusting entries fall between the unadjusted and adjusted trial balance at period-end, right before the financial statements.
  • Accrual basis (GAAP) recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred — cash timing does not drive recognition. Cash basis is only for small private firms.
  • The time period concept is why adjustments exist: over an infinite horizon cash and accrual totals match; periodic reporting forces end-of-period updates for events with no daily transaction (insurance expiring, revenue earned over time).
  • Cash-timing hook: Deferrals = cash FIRST, recognize later; Accruals = recognize FIRST, cash later. Four types: deferred revenue, deferred expense, accrued revenue, accrued expense.
  • The professor's core rule: every adjusting entry is recorded at period-end, hits exactly one income-statement account and exactly one balance-sheet account, and NEVER involves Cash.
  • Revenue Recognition (ASC 606): 5 steps — identify contract, identify performance obligations, set transaction price, allocate price, recognize as each obligation is satisfied.
  • Matching principle: match expenses to the period they helped earn revenue — about timing, not equal dollar amounts.
  • Depreciation: cost is allocated to expense over time via Depreciation Expense, credited to the contra-asset Accumulated Depreciation; the asset cost itself is never reduced.

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Supplies Expense = Beginning balance + Purchases − Ending physical count
  • Straight-line depreciation = (Cost − Residual) ÷ Useful life — numerator is the Depreciation Base (not Book Value)
  • Book Value = Cost − Accumulated Depreciation
  • Interest = Principal × Rate × Time — rate is always annual; multiply by the fraction of the year (× 1/12 for one month)
  • Partial-period depreciation = annual amount ÷ 12 × months
  • Step-4 allocation: split a lump-sum price by each item's share of total standalone value

Must-Know Journal Entries

  • Deferred revenue: Dr. Unearned Revenue / Cr. Revenue
  • Deferred (prepaid) expense: Dr. Expense / Cr. Prepaid Asset (supplies: Dr. Supplies Expense / Cr. Supplies)
  • Depreciation: Dr. Depreciation Expense / Cr. Accumulated Depreciation
  • Accrued revenue: Dr. Accounts Receivable / Cr. Revenue
  • Accrued expense: Dr. Expense / Cr. Payable (e.g., Dr. Interest Expense / Cr. Interest Payable)

Common Mistakes

  • Putting Cash in an adjusting entry — the biggest red flag; if Cash appears, it's a transaction, not an adjustment.
  • Forgetting the rate is annual — multiply by the fraction of the year; likewise divide annual depreciation by 12 for partial periods.
  • Writing the same account on both sides ("Insurance / Insurance") — separate the expense (I/S) from the asset (B/S).
  • Crediting the Equipment account directly for depreciation — credit Accumulated Depreciation instead.
  • Confusing Depreciation Base (Cost − Salvage) with Book Value (Cost − Accumulated Depreciation).
  • Treating the initial cash transaction as the adjustment — the adjustment happens only at period-end.
  • Misclassifying accounts — Unearned Revenue is a liability; Accumulated Depreciation is a contra-asset with a credit balance.
  • Mixing up deferral vs. accrual direction — check whether cash came first (deferral) or recognition first (accrual).
  • Wrong omission impact — omitting a deferred expense overstates assets, understates expenses, and overstates net income/equity.

Exam Checklist

  • ☐ You should be able to differentiate cash-basis from accrual-basis and explain why GAAP requires accrual.
  • ☐ You should be able to apply the time period concept, the 5-step revenue recognition principle (incl. price allocation), and the matching principle.
  • ☐ You should be able to classify a situation into one of the four categories using the cash-timing hook.
  • ☐ You should be able to journalize the adjusting entry for each type and verify it against the three rules (period-end, one I/S + one B/S account, no Cash).
  • ☐ You should be able to compute amounts: months elapsed × per-month rate; supplies from beginning + purchases − ending count; straight-line depreciation (annual and partial-period); interest = principal × annual rate × fraction of year.
  • ☐ You should be able to distinguish supplies (credit the asset directly) from depreciation (credit Accumulated Depreciation) and compute Book Value.
  • ☐ You should be able to prepare/interpret an adjusted trial balance and trace an account from unadjusted → adjustment → adjusted, confirming debits = credits.
  • ☐ You should be able to analyze the impact of recording vs. omitting an adjusting entry on assets, liabilities, equity, revenues/expenses, and net income.

Chapter 4 — Completing the Accounting Cycle

📖 For worked examples and full detail, see the Study Guide — Chapter 4.

Reversing entries (appendix) WILL NOT be tested — skip them.

Key Ideas

  • Temporary accounts (Revenues, Expenses, Dividends, Income Summary) are closed to zero each period so the income statement measures only one period's activity. Permanent accounts (Assets, Liabilities, Common Stock, Retained Earnings) carry forward and are never closed.
  • Income Summary is a temporary holding account used only during closing; it has no normal balance, appears on no statement, and its balance after closing revenues/expenses equals Net Income (credit) or Net Loss (debit).
  • The four closing steps: (1) close revenues to Income Summary, (2) close expenses to Income Summary, (3) close Income Summary to Retained Earnings, (4) close Dividends directly to Retained Earnings — never through Income Summary (dividends aren't expenses).
  • Retained Earnings is permanent — closing updates it (adds net income, subtracts dividends) but never resets it to zero. The adjusted trial balance shows beginning R/E; only after closing does it show ending R/E.
  • The post-closing trial balance is the final step of the cycle: prepared after closing, it contains only permanent accounts (no revenues, expenses, dividends, or Income Summary).
  • Classified balance sheet: assets listed by liquidity, liabilities by maturity. Current vs. long-term split applies to assets and liabilities but not to equity.
  • Current = realized/settled within 12 months OR the operating cycle, whichever is longer. PP&E must be long-lived, tangible, and used in operations (intent governs — speculative land is a long-term investment, not PP&E).
  • Financial-statement order (interrelated): Adjusted Trial Balance → Income Statement → Statement of Retained Earnings → Balance Sheet. Preparing statements is NOT the last step — you still close and run the post-closing trial balance.

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Ending R/E = Beginning R/E + Net Income − Dividends (subtract a net loss)
  • Net Income = Total Revenues − Total Expenses (= Income Summary balance after Steps 1–2)
  • Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities (both point-in-time; no averaging, unlike ROA). Prefer > 1.0.
  • Current classification threshold = 12 months OR operating cycle, whichever is longer.
  • Closing-direction rules (hit the opposite of the normal balance to zero it out):
  • Revenue: normal credit → debit to close
  • Expense: normal debit → credit to close
  • Dividends: normal debit → credit to close
  • Closing targets: Revenues & Expenses → Income Summary; Income Summary → Retained Earnings; Dividends → Retained Earnings directly.

Must-Know Journal Entries

Four closing entries (generic pattern):

  1. Close revenues: Dr. Revenue / Cr. Income Summary
  2. Close expenses (one compound entry for all): Dr. Income Summary / Cr. Expenses
  3. Close Income Summary: Dr. Income Summary / Cr. Retained Earnings (net income) — flip for a net loss: Dr. Retained Earnings / Cr. Income Summary
  4. Close dividends (direct): Dr. Retained Earnings / Cr. Dividends

Common Mistakes

  • Thinking the financial statements are the last step — you must still close and prepare the post-closing trial balance.
  • Routing Dividends through Income Summary — they close directly to R/E (they're not expenses).
  • Closing permanent accounts — only Rev, Exp, Dividends, and Income Summary are closed; never assets, liabilities, Common Stock, or R/E.
  • Treating Retained Earnings as temporary / resetting it to zero — it's permanent; closing only updates it.
  • Copying adjusted-T/B Retained Earnings straight onto the balance sheet — that's the beginning balance; prepare the Statement of R/E first to get the ending balance.
  • Expecting the worksheet's Income Statement columns to balance — the debit/credit gap is net income; don't "fix" it.
  • Putting temporary accounts on the post-closing trial balance — permanent accounts only.
  • Mixing up close directions — Revenue (credit normal) is debited; Expenses & Dividends (debit normal) are credited.
  • Using "whichever is shorter" — current classification uses 12 months OR operating cycle, whichever is longer.
  • Misclassifying speculative land as PP&E — if not used in operations, it's a long-term investment (intent governs).
  • Averaging current-ratio inputs — both are point-in-time figures (averaging belongs to ROA).
  • Reading a current ratio < 1.0 as automatic bankruptcy — it's a liquidity flag, not a death sentence.
  • Studying reversing entries — explicitly not tested.

Exam Checklist

  • ☐ You should be able to recite the financial-statement order (Adj. T/B → I/S → Stmt of R/E → Balance Sheet) and what flows between them (Net Income; Ending R/E).
  • ☐ You should be able to list temporary vs. permanent accounts from memory (Income Summary = temporary; R/E = permanent).
  • ☐ You should be able to perform all four closing steps with correct Dr./Cr. directions and targets (Dividends → R/E directly).
  • ☐ You should be able to identify Income Summary's post-Step-2 balance as Net Income (Cr.) or Net Loss (Dr.), and flip Step 3 for a net loss.
  • ☐ You should be able to compute Ending R/E = Beg + N.I. − Dividends and tie it to the closed R/E T-account.
  • ☐ You should be able to state what does/doesn't appear on a post-closing trial balance (permanent only; R/E = ending).
  • ☐ You should be able to classify accounts on a classified balance sheet (current vs. long-term; assets by liquidity, liabilities by maturity; no split for equity).
  • ☐ You should be able to apply the "12 months OR operating cycle, whichever is longer" rule.
  • ☐ You should be able to distinguish PP&E from a long-term investment by intent / use in operations.
  • ☐ You should be able to compute and interpret the Current Ratio (CA ÷ CL; > 1.0 preferred; no average).
  • ☐ You should be able to recall the worksheet quirk: I/S columns don't balance; the gap = Net Income.
  • ☐ You should be able to order the full accounting cycle from beginning balances to post-closing trial balance.
