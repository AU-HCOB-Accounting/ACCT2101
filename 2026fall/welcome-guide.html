⚠️ Why the First Four Chapters Are Everything

Chapters 1 through 4 Are the Foundation of This Entire Course

Every topic in this course builds on the concepts introduced in the first four chapters. If you struggle with Chapters 1–4, you will struggle with every chapter that follows. There is no way to catch up later—these concepts are cumulative.

Chapters 1 & 2 are especially critical. They introduce the Accounting Equation (A = L + E), double-entry bookkeeping, debits & credits, journal entries, and T-accounts. These are the building blocks of everything in accounting.

Your 1st Exam is Tuesday, September 15, covering Chapters 1 & 2. The Ch 1-2 Practice and Ch 1-2 Test tools (100 points each) are both due the night before.

I am not exaggerating: students who invest serious time in Chapters 1–2 during the first few weeks almost always perform well for the rest of the course. A 15-week semester feels roomy in August — it is not, once the chapters start stacking up.

🧠 Ch 1-2 Practice — 100 points just for doing the work

Open it here: ch1-2-practice.html

You are not graded on whether you get the answers right. You are graded on how much of it you complete. Get every question wrong and still finish everything — you earn all 100 points.

What "complete" means: each of the 180 questions must be answered twice — once in Practice (no timer) and once in Timed Practice. That is 180 × 2 = 360 completions for full marks.

  • Your score is simply the share you finish. Complete 288 of 360 and you earn 80 points.
  • A question only counts when you choose an answer. If the timer runs out and you have not selected anything, it does not count — you will see it again.
  • The tool always serves you the questions you have not done yet first, so you never waste time.
  • Progress saves automatically after every answer. Stop and come back as often as you like — but use the same computer and browser, and never private/incognito mode.
  • A progress bar at the top always shows exactly where you stand out of 360.

Timer in Timed Practice: 3 seconds for Modules 1 & 3, 15 seconds for Module 2.

🔄 Accounting Cycle Simulator for Ch 1-4 — Learn by Doing

What Is It?

The Accounting Cycle Simulator for Ch 1-4 is an interactive tool I built specifically for this course. It follows one company from its first transaction through year-end closing, letting you see in real time how each transaction affects the accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity).

Instead of just reading about how transactions work in a textbook, you can experience them. This is one of the most effective ways to understand the foundation of double-entry bookkeeping. The Simulator covers transactions through Chapter 4 (Completing the Accounting Cycle), including closing entries. I strongly recommend using it repeatedly as you progress through Chapters 1–4 to build and reinforce your intuition.

How to Use It

  1. Open it in Chrome or Edge: Accounting Cycle Simulator for Ch 1-4 (also on the course website).
  2. Enter the password: ACCT2101B
  3. Work through the guided transactions and observe how each one changes the equation.
  4. Pay attention to which accounts are affected and whether they increase or decrease.
Note: This tool is available through December 1, 2026 (the day of our final exam). After that the file will no longer open. Take advantage of it while you can.

Tip: Use the Simulator alongside your Chapter 1 & 2 study. After watching a lecture or reading about a concept, try it in the Simulator to reinforce your understanding.

⚖️ Accounting Equation Simulator — Practice Every Chapter

What Is It?

Building on the Accounting Cycle Simulator for Ch 1-4, the Accounting Equation Simulator is a set of chapter-by-chapter simulators that cover the journal entries and ledger postings for the entire course (Chapters 1–13). Choose a simulator (Ch 1–2, Ch 3–4, Ch 5–8, or Ch 9–13), pick a worked example, and step through each transaction to see how it changes the accounting equation, how it is journalized, how it posts to the T-accounts, and how it shapes the financial statements.

These are ideal for exam preparation: each exam’s chapters have their own examples — purchases & sales, inventory costing (FIFO/LIFO/Weighted-Average), depreciation methods, bonds, stock issuance, dividends, and more.

How to Use It

  1. Open it here in Chrome or Edge: Accounting Equation Simulator (also linked in the sidebar of every textbook page).
  2. Enter the password: ACCT2101B
  3. Pick a Simulator → a Chapter → an Example, then click Next (or use the ←/→ arrow keys) to step through.
  4. A 🔗 marks a linked sequence — transactions that build on one another (e.g., a purchase and its later payment or adjustment).
Note: Like the other tools, this is available through December 1, 2026.
